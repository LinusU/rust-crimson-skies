## The stated rules

Applied in order; the first rule whose conditions the record's keys satisfy
assigns the family. No rule invents a family for a record that states none —
it becomes `t452_01.limit.<record>`.

| rule | the record's keys state | family |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `t452_01.rule.torpedo` | `TORPEDO` | `aerial_torpedo` |
| `t452_01.rule.guided` | `LOCK_ON` | `guided_rocket` |
| `t452_01.rule.effect` | `TANGLER`, `SMOKE_SCREEN`, `FLASH`, `SONIC`, `BEEPER` or `BEEPER_SEEKER` | `area_denial_engine` |
| `t452_01.rule.airburst` | `IMPACT_PROXIMITY` or `DETONATION_DISTANCE`, unguided | `proximity_flak` |
| `t452_01.rule.direct` | `ROCKET` | `direct_explosive` |

The ordering is load-bearing: the torpedo declares `LOCK_ON` **and** `TORPEDO`
(it is the aerodynamic weapon, not an ordinary seeker), and the guided flash
and sonic records declare `LOCK_ON` **and** their effect flags (they are
seekers with an effect payload). `TORPEDO` then `LOCK_ON` then the effect
flags is the only order consistent with what the keys say.

`cs_content` may not link `cs_sim`, so the classification lands on
`DeclaredOrdnanceFamily`; `accept_t452_01_` pins it against
`cs_sim::weapons::ordnance::OrdnanceFamily` variant-for-variant on the
test-only edge where both crates are visible.

### What the census found that the rocket reader could not

- **`wep_25` is a thirteenth `LOCK_ON` record.** The glidebomb
  (`MSG_WEAP_GLIDEBOMB`, `NAME "gb"`) declares `LOCK_ON 3`, `LOCK_ON_LEAD`,
  `DETONATION_DISTANCE`, `IMPACT_PROXIMITY 155` and `GRAVITY` — and **no
  `ROCKET` flag**. `read_original_rocket_blasts` structurally cannot see it,
  so the twelve-record guided census of `accept_t454_`/`accept_t548_` (which
  is of `ROCKET` records only) never reached it. With `wep_25` the member
  carries thirteen `LOCK_ON` records: the twelve `ROCKET`-flagged ones plus
  the glidebomb.
- **`wep_26` is a self-named fake weapon** (`MSG_WEAP_FAKE_WEAPON`,
  `NAME "FW"`): `ROCKET` + `IMPACT_PROXIMITY 100`, unguided, zero damage —
  airburst-shaped by its keys. Whether anything launches it is not a record
  question.
- **`wep_28` is a cannonball** (`MSG_WEAP_CANNONBALL`): `ACCELERATION`,
  `GRAVITY`, `CLUSTER_SIZE`, `CRATER`, `DAMAGES_ZEPPELIN`, `RANGE 3500` — a
  ballistic projectile shape with neither `ROCKET` nor `CANNON`. No stated
  rule covers it: `t452_01.limit.wep_28`.
- **Fifteen `ROCKET` records against eleven selectable types** — recorded as
  `rocket_type_count_mismatch { rocket_records: 15, screen_types: 11 }`, not
  reconciled: the screens enumerate the *selectable* rocket types and the
  member also carries the torpedo, the flare, the smoke and the fake record,
  so the two numbers measure different things. `wep_25`'s absence from the
  fifteen is a second witness that `ROCKET` and "the engine's ordnance" are
  not the same set.
- **`direct_explosive` and `nitro_booster` are declared families with no
  record.** Every damaging `ROCKET` record is guided, airburst or an effect;
  the nitro control the installation names (`MPOUT_CHK_NITRO`, F28-D) has no
  `wep_` record at all — it is equipment, not a projectile, consistent with
  the boost contract ("equipment/state", not a fuse-carrying item).

## Test sensitivity

The classification is a pure function (`classify_original_weapons` needs no
installation), so each rule is pinned on hand-built records including the two
ordering traps (`TORPEDO`+`LOCK_ON` → torpedo; `LOCK_ON`+`FLASH` → guided).
The retail tests call `read_original_weapon_census` and
`read_original_weapon_records` directly: removing the reader fails to
compile, not just to pass. `accept_t452_01_retail_*` are
`#[ignore = "requires CS_GAME_DIR"]` and fail loudly without it.
